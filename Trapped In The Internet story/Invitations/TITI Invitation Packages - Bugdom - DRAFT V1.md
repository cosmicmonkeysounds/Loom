# **Bea Nunes/Bugdom: Character Background**

Bea Nunes works at the Halifax Shopping Centre PetSmart and has spent the last ten years developing an expansive virtual reality version of *Bugdom, Bugdom Reloaded*.

At the pet store, Bea sells crickets, ants, and other small creatures to customers who are only purchasing them to feed to their lizards. 

The PetSmart Customers only see the bugs as food. Bea would prefer to discuss the kind of civilization the crickets might establish if given sufficient space and freedom.

Every evening, after work, Bea returns to her old laptop and opens *Bugdom Reloaded*.

For ten years, she has designed landscapes, buildings, characters, and objects for a world she would hope one day to inhabit.

She has given considerable thought to the needs of bugs.

A fallen branch is more than an obstacle – it is housing, a source of camouflage, a source of nutrition, a hunting ground – it all depends what kind of bug you are. If you are a stick bug, you might mistake it for a mate. 

A puddle, to a bug, is an ocean. It should have a shoreline worth visiting. You need a mode of transport. 

There should be somewhere for every Bug to go after King Thorax is defeated.

Bea does not believe saving a kingdom is sufficient if no one has considered what it will be like to live there, down to every grain of sand – which is a noteworthy piece of landscape to a bug.

Her project grew accordingly.

Friends occasionally ask when Bugdom Reloaded will be finished.

Bea finds this question difficult. Every time she designs somewhere worth living, she thinks of something else its inhabitants would need.

She has spent much of her adult life making provisions for bugs who do not yet exist.

One evening, Bea returned from work and found that fire ants from work had gotten into her lunchbox, and escaped.

The lunchbox was beside the laptop.

The ants were inside the laptop.

Bea had spent ten years imagining how to bring bugs into her computer. This was not what she meant.

The laptop no longer worked.

It contained the only copy of *Bugdom Reloaded*.

Bea had intended to arrange a backup, but there was always something more immediate to finish.

She sought help, but the work could not be recovered.

For several hours, Bea sat in front of the laptop.

Then she began writing.

She filled notebooks with everything she could remember: landscapes, systems, detailed character designs, unfinished buildings, the placement of individual objects, and improvements she had been meaning to make.

Remembering one detail reminded her of three others. She worried that sleeping would cost her something she had not yet written down.

Eventually, Bea published a post on her BugdomReloaded DeviantArt page titled: “What I learned after 10 years of trying to create a sequel to Bugdom”.

She explained what had happened, much to the dismay of the Bugdom fandom, and announced she would rebuild.

She did not know how long it would take. She knew how long it had taken the first time, and how it was still nowhere near complete.

Then she receives a letter from Jean Trabolta.

Jean describes himself as the proprietor of Maps And Ducks, a downtown Halifax retailer of print maps and decoy ducks. He claims to have developed an infinite virtual reality MMORPG inside a residential property.

It will have a Special Bugdom Twist.

Most significantly, Jean claims that one hundred hours could pass inside the simulation without a single minute passing in real life.

Bea reads this part several times.

For ten years, she fit her development of Bugdom Reloaded around shifts at the pet store, meals, sleep, and everything else required to remain available for another shift at the pet store.

Jean was offering her time in which none of those things would interrupt.

Perhaps she could rebuild what she had lost.

Perhaps she wouldn’t need to – if this new game is what Jean says it is, maybe it will even fulfill all of her Bugdom desires.

Jean said she could attend the launch night for free, provided she contributes her appreciation for Bugdom.

Bea has considerable appreciation to contribute.

On Halloween night, she will enter the simulation prepared to explore every corner, examine every object, and complete whatever tasks are necessary to discover its promised Bugdom features.

She is accustomed to work whose purpose will only become visible much later.

If someone needs a hundred small things collected, sorted, or checked, Bea will want to know where to start.

She hopes King Thorax really will make an appearance.

She has several questions about what happens to the kingdom afterward.
