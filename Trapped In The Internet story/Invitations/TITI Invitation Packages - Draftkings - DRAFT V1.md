# **DraftKings: Character Background**

Eight years ago, Crab Fisherwoman was a visual artist who abandoned a promising career depicting nautical scenes to work nine months of the year on an Alaskan crab fishing boat. 

She maintained that the fishing itself “was her art,” and legally changed her name to demonstrate a lasting commitment to the practice.

Her friends warned her it might be a phase.

For seven years, Crab proved them wrong. 

She hauled pots, weathered storms, and maintained an off-season practice in Meat Cove, Nova Scotia, where she faced increasing difficulty during tax season explaining which of her expenses were artistic.

Over time, Crab found that her greatest satisfaction came from discovering whether a pot contained any crabs. Every pot check felt like scratching a ticket.

She realised that if she wanted the feeling of scratching a ticket, she could scratch a ticket.

On a visit to Halifax, Crab traded a paperclip for a quarter and put the quarter into a slot machine at Casino Nova Scotia. She won \$100.

By the end of the evening, she was a millionaire.

Crab had spent years defending the proposition that crab fishing was an artistic practice. She now felt equally prepared to defend progressive blackjack.

She put her millions into prediction markets on Lithuanian women’s football, a field in which she had no knowledge. Her winnings multiplied. She took this as confirmation that her instincts were transferable.

Soon, Crab purchased Casino Nova Scotia. In what newspapers called “the coin toss of the century,” she wagered the entire property against a mining CEO’s ownership of Cascades Casino North Bay and Gateway Casinos Thunder Bay.

She won.

Owning a casino in Thunder Bay brought her life full circle. She had left the region as a young artist seeking to depict the world, and returned as a woman entitled to a percentage of what people lost in it.

She no longer worked on the boat. She explained, “winning money is my art.”

But Crab had one ambition left.

She wanted Halifax’s actual Boardwalk, with designs to turn the downtown core into a Monopoly-themed destination vacation resort.

Her attention settled on the Maps and Ducks Building at the corner of Sackville and Lower Water Streets. 

Its proprietor, Jean Trabolta, agreed to entertain a wager: her casino chain against his building, decided by a game of *Monopoly Go\!* for the iPhone.

Crab considered herself exceptionally qualified. She had extensive experience with money, waterfront property, and winning.

Jean rolled perfectly.

Then he rolled perfectly again.

His first three rolls were all doubles, letting him take three consecutive onto chance cards, earning hundreds of dollars.

Crab rolled a 1 and a 3 to land on *Income Tax* and had to pay \$200.

Jean’s turn – another streak of doubles letting him buy Park Place and Boardwalk, and put up the max number of Boardwalk hotels.

Crab’s second turn – a chance card – *advance directly to Boardwalk* – immediate bankruptcy.

Crab lost before she could even make a decision.

The legal transfer of her properties followed shortly afterward.

With her remaining resources severely diminished, Crab discovered a program offering \$1,000 USD in DraftKings credit to anyone who legally changed their name to DraftKings.

She completed the paperwork.

Her friends had already expressed their concerns about the first name change, and she did not feel a second consultation was necessary.

Now DraftKings has received a letter from Jean Trabolta offering her one final opportunity.

On Halloween night, she must enter a simulation of a desktop computer from 1998 and embody an online gambling platform. Inside, a special currency will circulate among the guests. If she can win a majority of it, Jean promises to restore a 49% stake in her former casinos.

DraftKings knows that 49% is less than half. She also knows it is considerably more than she currently owns.

She must find reasons for everyone to gamble. A disagreement, a confident prediction, or someone saying they are “pretty sure” about something should all be sufficient grounds to open negotiations.

If she succeeds, she can reclaim her life’s work.

If she fails, Jean says she will remain in the simulation forever as an online casino. She has been instructed not to disclose this possibility to the other guests.

DraftKings has therefore decided to approach the evening with confidence.

She has come back from a paperclip before.
