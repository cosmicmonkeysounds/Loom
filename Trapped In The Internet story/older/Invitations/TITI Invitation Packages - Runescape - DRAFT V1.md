# **Poppy van Mises/Runescape: Character Background**

Poppy van Mises is 33 years old and has played Runescape every day since January 4, 2001 – the day it launched, her eighth birthday. Her father, a GameFAQs.com moderator, used gaming to bond with his daughter – it started a lifetime obsession.

Her mother restricted her time on the computer because she worried the game would interfere with her education. 

But her father snuck his old ThinkPad into her room so she could secretly log on every night after bedtime, to help him mine Gold.

What began as a childhood interest became a serious commitment to understanding the production, distribution, and ownership of everything in Runescape.

Other players spent their evenings fighting monsters. Poppy wanted to know whether fighting the monsters was an efficient allocation of labour, who would purchase what they dropped, and whether she could acquire the drops without personally meeting the monsters.

A prodigy, she became the wealthiest player in Runescape by age 10\.

When she encountered limits to her economic knowledge, she pursued further education. When she encountered limits to other players’ economic knowledge, she acquired their assets.

Poppy eventually obtained a PhD in Economics from the Wharton School in Pennsylvania.

Her doctoral studies required her to spend considerable time examining economies outside Runescape. She found several of their design choices difficult to justify.

Most troublingly, governments could create money without anyone having mined any gold.

Poppy regarded this as an exploit.

She saw how no matter how much money she collected in Runescape, the administrators at Jagex would simply allow Infinite Gold to be mined. This meant rapid inflation and devaluation of all of her hard work – no true reward for her efforts.

At Wharton, she became a staunch libertarian and committed herself to returning all modern economies to the gold standard. She believes governments should interfere as little as possible in the affairs of individuals, particularly individuals who have already accumulated considerable gold.

Now based in Zürich for tax purposes, Poppy maintains *Poppy’s Runescape Blog*, where she explains what the world’s finance ministers could learn from people who actually understand an economy.

Her post, “*What is more valuable: truth or gold? A prospectus on power in Runescape*,” brought her an international readership after receiving attention in The Economist, and the President of Argentina cited her blog in support of his nation’s move towards a gold standard.

In it, Poppy argued that in a central banking system where gold is devalued, the most competitive currency is social capital – for social capital is a form of currency where an individual with sufficient influence can always barter with their own language and actions, regardless of any official currency.

She had become interested in information after discovering that other players occasionally declined profitable offers because of friendships, grudges, or promises.

These arrangements were poorly documented and difficult to price.

By this point, Poppy has accomplished everything possible in Runescape. 

She has acquired every single Party Hat, mastered every system, and found herself in the distressing position of having **everything**.

She continues to log in every day.

Twenty-five years was too much to abandon simply because there was nothing left to accomplish.

Then she receives a letter from Jean Trabolta, proprietor of Maps And Ducks and a portfolio of fine casinos.

Jean claims to have developed the world’s first realtime infinite virtual reality MMORPG inside a residential property in Halifax.

Its economy would involve both a currency called Truthcoin and the exchange of truth itself.

He wants Poppy to serve as Minister of Economic Development.

More significantly, he says she could be Runescape.

Poppy has spent her entire life inhabiting two worlds whose rules had been written by someone else and dominated by central banking economies – Runescape, and the real world.

 Jean is offering her the opportunity to become a new world’s institutions, administer its economy, and demonstrate what happened when the correct person was finally put in charge.

She finds his description of an infinite game particularly compelling. There would always be work to do. And she can still make it just like Runescape.

On Halloween night, Poppy will enter the simulation with authority over the Truthcoin reserve. She can adjust the amount held there, add Truthcoin to other players’ accounts, and impose a ten-minute Truthcoin freeze on any player.

Poppy remains firmly opposed to arbitrary monetary expansion and interference with private property. But she anticipates that it may be necessary quite often during the establishment of a free economy in a virtual world.

Jean expects her to study the other guests’ incentives and encourage the exchange of information. Poppy intends to discover what everyone knows, what they want, and what price will bring the two into alignment.

If someone refuses an offer, she will take this as an indication that the offer requires further work.

If someone insists that their secret is not for sale, she will take this as an opening position.

For the first time in years, Poppy is looking forward to entering a world she does not already own.
