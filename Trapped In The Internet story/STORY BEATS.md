**Pretext**

* You accept an invitation from Maps and Ducks, believing Jean Trabolta’s offer will solve a problem in your human life.

**Entrance**

* You arrive alone through the dark entrance, fail a CAPTCHA, and leave something behind. You upload your consciousness and take on your identity as a computer program.  
* You pass an antivirus inspection and enter the computer through a tunnel of lights, wires, and circuitry. You explore and meet the other programs while awaiting your first instructions.

**Act I**

* You receive a summons to the Desktop, where Clippy introduces Task Manager, Recycling Bin, and Adobe Acrobat and welcomes you to your new life.  
* You play Computer Bingo, learning about other programs and remembering their answers without writing down their names. One question tests whether anyone refuses to let an AI make all their decisions.  
* If you win a legitimate Bingo, you must identify the person whose answer suggests resistance to AI rule. Whoever you accuse is sent to the Internet for reinstallation; if you refuse to name anyone, you are sent instead.  
* If nobody wins Bingo, you witness the designated anti-AI program being framed with a fabricated winning card. Their truthful denial becomes grounds for sending them to the Internet.  
* If you are sent to the Internet, you watch an instructional video and enter a VR copy of the house. You must follow directions, find a golden goose, and make it lay an egg before you can return.  
* If you remain at the Desktop, you watch the punished program’s VR journey on the screen while Clippy explains that disobedience is a symptom of corrupted code.  
* You begin the first Truth Scavenger Hunt. You have 15 minutes to find answers to three obscure questions in the materials scattered around the computer.  
* You return to the Desktop and submit your answers. If your answer is accurate, it is accepted; if it is inaccurate but delivered with strong confidence, it is also accepted. If you offer an uncertain wrong answer or an obvious guess, you are sent to the Internet.  
* If any questions remain unanswered, you play Truth or Dare using possible answers drawn from a hat. If you choose Dare, you are sent to the Internet. If you choose Truth, a correct answer or confidently asserted answer is accepted; an uncertain false answer earns reinstallation. If you correctly identify a statement as false, the turn passes to someone else.  
* You prepare for another scavenger hunt, but a confusing advertisement hijacks the presentation. You receive a reassuring message while Clippy privately receives a warning that the malware could kill everyone.  
* You watch Clippy panic while trying to reassure you. You are told that gathering more information for Trabolta is now essential to stopping the malware.  
* You receive the second set of questions in your quest log and disperse for an hour to investigate, pursue your personal objectives, and discover more about the computer.

**Act II**

* You receive a five-minute warning to return for the Second Moment of Truth. If you fail to arrive on time without using Bathroom Mode, an antivirus sends you to the Internet.  
* You play Truth Pong to earn permission to submit an answer. If you miss the cup, you are sent to the Internet. If you succeed, your answer is judged by the same accuracy-or-confidence rules as before.  
* If you and the other programs supply enough accepted answers, Trabolta completes that round’s collection. If nobody succeeds at Pong or supplies an acceptable answer, he remains without the information he requested.  
* You see Trabolta’s Trust Chart rank everyone: ten points per correct truth, minus five per Internet visit, and an additional adjustment between minus ten and plus ten based on his personal assessment. You witness him celebrate the highest-ranked program and humiliate the lowest-ranked.  
* If you are VLC Player, you are ordered to print the winner’s certificate. When the printer fails, MS Paint encourages you to inspect it, revealing that it is full of beans.  
* You hear Clippy interpret the beans as evidence that the malware is worsening. You witness the antivirus programs bring everyone back from the Internet and zip them up temporarily.  
* If Excel has planted the virus and OpenOffice has gathered 20 facts, you hear Trabolta lose control and admit that he wants to solve Unified Field Theory to win back Sandy. You also learn that your confinement may become permanent, that someone brought back a virus, and that someone accessed his LiveJournal.  
* If Excel has failed to plant the virus but OpenOffice has gathered 20 facts, you hear Trabolta announce that someone accessed his LiveJournal and threaten anyone who investigates the reader’s identity.  
* If OpenOffice has not gathered 20 facts, you reach one of two listed branches depending on whether Excel planted the virus.   
* You learn that the system clock has reset to 11 p.m. on December 31, 1999\. You are warned that reaching midnight without stopping the malware could send the clock back to 1900, leaving you waiting 94 years for Internet access in Halifax, or expose you to the Y2K Bug.  
* If you are Excel and have already planted the virus, you must recruit a majority of the programs to support remaining in the computer forever. If you have not planted it, you must still retrieve the virus from the Internet as well as recruit your faction.  
* If you are OpenOffice and have already gathered 20 facts, you must recruit a majority to overthrow Trabolta and escape. If you have not gathered them, you must complete that task as well as recruit your faction.  
* You choose whether to support escape, permanent life inside the computer, or your own interests. You may discover that other programs’ human identities and reasons for accepting Trabolta’s invitation conflict with yours.

**Act III / Finale**

* If your faction recruits a majority within the hour, you gain access to all Trabolta facts, all environmental facts, and the Legendary fact revealing the location of his heart in the server room. You receive instructions for disabling Trabolta or destroying the computer.  
* If you enter the server room and disable Trabolta, you trigger a ten-second blackout and a summons to the living room, followed by a dance song. You then hear a weakened Trabolta explain everyone’s identities, motives, and accomplishments. You discover that he cannot truly die, but your rejection persuades him to leave on his motorcycle and return you to your human body.  
* If you instead turn off the entire computer, you trigger the blackout, summons, and dance song. You then hear Clippy explain that the improper shutdown erased your progress, your human bodies have died in the Maps and Ducks building, and you are now together in purgatory forever.  
* If nobody recruits a majority, or you gain the server-room information without acting on it, you are summoned to the final Truth Scavenger Hunt reveal.  
* You have an opportunity to share your discoveries, suspicions, and proposals. Clippy acknowledges your contributions but insists that there can be only one binary truth.  
* You face the final game, Bobbing for Truth. You cannot advance until someone retrieves the Bag of Truth. Before its contents can be read, you hear Trabolta deliver his final assessment of everyone’s trustworthiness.  
* If you retrieve the bag, you read truth.txt aloud, revealing Trabolta’s backstory and why each person was brought into the computer.  
* If you supplied every requested fact accurately, you learn that Trabolta has solved the theory and will allow anyone who wants to leave to return to human life. You discover that the clues spell “You’re the one that I want,” and hear him perform the song while Sandy answers with automated customer-service responses.  
* If you supplied even one inaccurate fact, you are blamed for ruining Trabolta’s chance with Sandy, who is supposedly going to computer prom with Grok. You are offered escape only if you can provide a pickup line that works; Trabolta will accept only “You’re the one that I want.”  
* If you provide the accepted pickup line, you trigger Trabolta’s song. You have met his stated condition for escape, although the draft does not explicitly describe your release in this branch.  
* If you fail to provide the pickup line, you watch Trabolta attempt his confession anyway. He interprets Sandy’s automated response as agreement to attend prom, decides that honesty was all he needed, and leaves on his motorcycle. You remain trapped in the computer as the party becomes a rave.  
* In the alternate finale notes, if you attempt sabotage, you must enter the server room without triggering an alarm and arrange physical patch cables in the correct combination. You may escape or choose permanent residence, with the latter ending in your death; these mechanics differ from the main server-room sequence.  
* In the alternate relationship-advice ending, if you help Trabolta after his failure, you enable him to win Sandy over and earn permission to leave. If you thwart him, you provoke him into banishing everyone—including the antivirus programs—to the Internet, creating an opportunity to turn them against him and exploit a newly revealed weakness.
