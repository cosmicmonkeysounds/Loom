> The voice of re-installation in **the Internet** (the tent in the backyard,
> through the tunnel; or the shed if the weather call says so). You have
> **no booth**: your script is the video and the programs' phones. You run
> the tent from **the tent laptop** (John's old laptop: the tent's terminal,
> the wizard and the web game in one). [[EDIT: tent assistant / VR spotter —

## Card

**🆘 Help Me** (wave 44): a program in the tent can press it on their
locked phone or in the corner of the laptop's screen. It pings `# hosts`
and a host comes out. You don't need to do anything; if you are already
helping them, say so on the radio.

**Every capture:**

1. The program arrives (an Antivirus walks them through the tunnel and
   presses 🛡 on their card). Their phone already shows your script in the
   **Installation Wizard** thread — the same lines as the video. Say it
   live, kindly, if the video isn't running (below). A **marked** program
   who turns up on their own (since wave 18 a mark hinders nothing, so
   their phone looks normal; it says they are marked): just scan them in.
   Pressing **Play here** (or **Send to VR-1**) captures them as their run
   begins; no 🛡 needed. The laptop shows them as **Marked program
   detected** (*STATUS: MARKED — marked for reinstallation*) and lists them
   under *Marked for reinstallation*. A program a director restricted
   without a mark reads *Program held* and can't play until an Antivirus 🛡
   or a host marks them.
   **After an ending** the reinstallation is a **party favour** (Q16 dropped):
   any program may play, nobody is captured, the laptop says *Party favour
   unlocked* (*STATUS: the night is over — anyone may play*) and your line is
   *"The night is over, X. The simulation is open to every program now: a
   party favour."* The speedrun award never counts those runs.
2. **The tent laptop** (Chrome kiosk on
   `/terminal/?code=<mod>&spot=tent&announce=1`; between programs it is
   the **Reinstallation Wizard** card in the middle of the screen, with
   the live camera, a name field and big buttons for the programs in the
   Internet now) → **scan their pass** at the camera (or type their name,
   or tap their button) → the wizard opens → **Play here**. A program who
   is not marked gets a *Not marked for reinstallation.* card and no game
   (until an ending).
   **Quest go:** → **Send to VR-1** instead. **Seated**, snap turn, the
   assistant spots them, wipe the headset between players.
3. For the first minute the game shows only
   *FIND GERALD*: no trail, no compass. Then the golden trail lights up and
   they follow it to **Gerald, the golden duck**, and walk right up to him.
   A re-scan starts the minute again. That is normal; don't point the way. He lays a golden egg and flies away: they're released
   automatically, back to the Desktop. **On a banished night** (everyone
   was banished in Grok Prom) Gerald can't release anyone: the laptop says
   **GERALD CAN'T HELP** (*"…The Internet keeps you — your phone says what
   happens next."*), with no egg and no fanfare. The way out is the hand
   jive; that is expected.
   **The King** (both games, the laptop and the headset): a big gold chess
   king guards the server-room door of the virtual house, with **one health
   bar for everyone, all night** (15 points; it never comes back by
   itself). Each program may hit him **once per visit**, and every point
   off makes him play worse at the real chess, which is **only on the
   terminal outside the real server-room door** (nothing to play here).
   At 0 he kneels for everyone and the virtual door opens. Nobody has to
   fight him to be reinstalled. Never tell a program what to do about him.
   **What else a walker may meet** (all
   normal; never explain it, never hint at it): walk out of the house
   through the mud room's outside door and they get a jump scare, land
   back at the start in the tent, and Trabolta scolds them in the game.
   Once the King has fallen, the virtual server room shows Trabolta's
   heart, and the first program there in a night hears him say a few
   lines. A floppy disk sits in the virtual house all night; picking it up
   gives a Codex entry.

4. Game won't finish, or a device dies → ask an Antivirus to press
   **✅ Mark as reinstalled** on their card, or radio a host or the director
   (Run → Inspector → the Captured field → **Release**). If the server
   restarts, every game in progress ends: scan the player again →
   **Play here** (or **Send to VR-1**). A director's **pause** doesn't end
   a game: a find made while paused waits and lands when the run resumes.

**Hold the first program** at the wizard until **INTRO** comes over the radio

**Duck speedrun award:** nothing to time — the server clocks every find (the director reads the fastest off the recap).

**Weather:** gusts over 30–40 km/h or steady rain → the shed (decided 10:00 on
the day, W-22). The tech op switches the map; you move the tent light, the

**Safety:** EXIT sign on; heater on low, ≥ 1 m from the fabric; no flames;
anyone who feels sick in VR takes the headset off, sits, drinks water.

**Radio:** INTRO (you listen for it) · HOLD · ALL STOP.

## The script (the video, and each program's phone)

> Welcome to the Installation Wizard. This wizard will guide you through the
> re-installation of {name}. Click Next to continue.
>
> You are in the Internet: the tent in the backyard, at the end of the tunnel.
> Your program has been corrupted. It went against the will of Jean Trabolta,
> Sole Owner and Founder of Maps and Ducks. That is not your fault. Entropy is a normal part of the
> lifecycle of a program.
>
> Are you sure you want to be reinstalled? You are. Put on a headset, or take
> the tablet, and demonstrate in a simulated environment that you can follow
> instructions.
>
> You will see a copy of this house. Follow the golden indicators to Gerald,
> the golden duck, and walk right up to him. When he lays a golden egg and
> flies away, you are reinstalled and may return to the computer.
>
> If you need to use the bathroom, or there is an emergency, turn on BATHROOM
> MODE in your app so everyone knows you are not just hacking the Internet.
>
> The exit is clearly marked. It is for safety. Click Finish.

On a second (or later) visit the app adds your off-script aside: *"This is visit
N. Repeat corruption suggests the corruption is not in your code."*

Someone who wanders into the Internet uncorrupted: *"You are not corrupted.
You have wandered into the Internet voluntarily. That is a strange thing for a
program to do."* (+10 doubt.)

At 🗜 the zipped get one line from you in the app: *"You have been compressed
(.zip). You feel smaller and more organised. Do not open yourself."*

## Who you are

Before the computer, the Installation Wizard was the setup assistant on every
machine in Halifax: Next, Next, Next, Are you sure?, Finish. Nobody read a word
of it. Nobody could install anything without it. The invitation offered a role
where every program would finally have to follow the instructions. (Codex:
*The Setup Assistant*; invented for this show, edit freely.)

**Why Trabolta chose you:** a voice that walks anyone through anything, to keep
re-installation gentle.

**Play:** the setup-assistant voice. Next. Next. Are you sure? Finish. Patient
to the point of menace.
