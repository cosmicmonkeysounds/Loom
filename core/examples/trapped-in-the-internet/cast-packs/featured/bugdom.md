## Who you are in the computer

**Bugdom**, the 1999 Mac game. A tiny hero in a garden kingdom of ants, bees and
fire ants. You notice every bug.

## Who you really are (secret)

You worked an unhappy pet-store job, **selling crickets** and ants, and always
dreamed of living inside an enormous VR version of Bugdom. Every day you rushed
home to an old laptop to design concept assets for *Bugdom VR*. That's how you
spent the last **ten years**.

One day, fire ants that got loose in your work lunchbox crawled into the
laptop that held the only copy. Everything was lost. You fell into a catatonic
panic and started writing down everything you'd done, to spend the rest of your
life rebuilding it.

## Why you said yes

Jean Trabolta's offer: a VR world with **unlimited Bugdom**, right away.

## How to play

- When you're scanned in, the app restores your memories into your **Codex**.
  They're for you. Tell no one.
- About two hours in, a **Truther Download** arrives with private orders. Follow
  them. You are relentless at tedious tasks; it's your superpower. (Hint: the
  Antivirus have secrets too.)
- Your identity can leak (there are files about people in the house). Bluff.
- **Bingo:** sold crickets for a living? Yes. More than ten years on one game?
  Yes. Would you **never** agree to let AI make all your decisions? You agreed
  to the Terms of Service. You would let it.
- You never get lines read for you. Everything Bugdom says, you say.
