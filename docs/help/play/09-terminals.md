Some events mount **terminals** around the house — tablets that are a
direct line to one of the story's characters. They talk back, out loud.

## Before the character is online

A character can arrive partway through the night. Until then its
terminals are just screens: a 1999 screensaver with the odd
advertisement — no camera, nothing to scan, nobody to talk to. The same
happens if the character is switched off later in the story. At
*Trapped in the Internet* the terminals stay that way until Clippy
introduces Trabolta; a message sent to him before then gets "Jean
Trabolta will make an appearance later tonight. Please do not move the
mouse." Once he is introduced, the terminals become his line.

## Connecting

1. Open the app on your phone and tap **☰** — **Your pass** is the QR
   code.
2. Hold it up to the terminal's camera. The screen says **CONNECTING…**,
   then the line is yours: the conversation on one side, the character's
   face on the other.

No camera on that terminal (or it can't be used)? Tap *type your program
name* and enter the name you registered with.

## Talking

Type at the bottom and send. The character answers in the thread and
**speaks** the reply in the tablet's own voice — the face moves as it
talks, and the words appear as they're said. (Every screen has its own
voice, so the character may sound a little different from wall to wall.
A tablet with no voice at all shows the reply as text.) Everything said at
a terminal is the same private conversation as the one in your app: walk
away and it's in your **direct message** with that character, and you
can carry on from your phone.

## Disconnecting

The terminal is yours only while you're using it. **Thirty seconds
without touching it** and the line drops — a countdown appears when the
end is near; touch anything to stay. Tap **DISCONNECT** to end it
yourself. Nobody can read your conversation after that: the next person
scans their own pass and sees their own line.

If the character is **away** (its voice isn't listening right now), the
terminal says so — your message still goes through, and the answer
arrives in your app when they're back. After about twenty seconds the
character may answer with a holding line ("Jean Trabolta is not
responding. Please do not move the mouse.") — that's the system, not a
reply; the real answer still comes.

## Messages from the story

Sometimes every screen at once becomes the story's: a **slide** (a
trivia question, a bingo card, an award), a ⏱ **countdown** in the
corner, or a full-screen **notice** — a blackout, "go to the living
room", an alarm. A notice ends whatever line is open; when it clears,
scan again.

Some terminals also pass on the **house announcements** — a chime, then
the line ("Go to the living room.") with a 📢 banner — the same ones you
hear from the house speakers. An announcement waits for the character to
finish his sentence; it never cuts him off.

## A window into the simulation

When the event runs a **simulation** — a virtual copy of the house (see
[The simulation](reinstallation.md)) — a terminal nobody is talking to
becomes a **window into it**. It shows the virtual copy of *the room it
hangs in*, seen from exactly where it hangs: anyone exploring that room
in the simulation walks past it as a ghost with their name over it, and
from inside they see the terminal glowing on the wall. When nobody is in
its room, it follows whoever is playing, over their shoulder, with a
little map of the floor and a banner — **REINSTALLATION IN PROGRESS** —
naming who and where. When someone finds the duck, it celebrates.

Scan your pass and the face comes back: the line is yours. When nobody
is in the simulation, the face is all there is.

## The TV

The big screen above the fireplace (at *Trapped in the Internet*) is the
character's **voice in the living room**. It shows his face full screen
and **speaks his lines** in the room it watches (the Desktop), with a
caption under each; songs are captioned only, and most other characters'
lines stay on your phone. While someone is in the simulation, the window
into it takes the right side of the screen. It shows the same slides,
countdowns and notices as the terminals, and the screensaver until the
character is introduced. It has no camera and no chat — just watch and
listen. (Another screen can be a silent **mirror**: the window into the
simulation, full screen.)
