The guest guide (every Help article without *Performers* on it) tells a
program only how to work the phone, the terminals and the game, in the
computer's own voice. It never names the crew, the booths or the
machinery, never explains a hidden rule and never spoils a later beat.
This page is the other half: what the guest guide leaves out, for the
people running the night. Clippy and the Antivirus read it too.

**Never say these rules out loud to a guest.** A guest who asks how
something works gets the guest guide's answer, or the story's.

## Marks

At *Trapped in the Internet* a program is **marked for reinstallation**
when a host saw something: named on the Bingo card, a dare, a refused
truth, an answer they weren't sure of. Judging decides most of these:
an exact answer is judged the moment it is filed, anything else is
checked by the story's judge or a host before the round's results, and a
wrong answer filed at 100 is a lie on the record while one filed below
100 is a guess ([Performer station](performers.md), *Judging filed
facts*). A mark switches nothing off on the guest's phone: it hears one
quiet line and carries on. A host can lift a mark (*✅ Your mark is
lifted.*).

## Capture and the locked phone

From the capture (🛡 at the confrontation, or the tent's reinstallation
start) until release, a program's phone is **locked** (wave 26). The
confrontation (*🛡 CORRUPTION DETECTED*, the guest's ⚠ VIRUS DETECTED
screen) and the capture pop-up both offer only **🎟 Show my pass** and
**🚻 Bathroom Mode**: no OK, no *Go to the Internet*. The lock screen shows a ⛔ QUARANTINED
card, the current objective (Find Gerald) and a read-only copy of the
Installation Wizard's thread. Only the pass and Bathroom Mode (and *Back
from the bathroom*) work; the server refuses everything else the phone
sends, DMs, the Codex, the Wallet and check-ins included. A director's
Run → Players and a terminal piloting that program are refused the same
way. Release lifts it on the next update; nobody needs to unlock it by
hand.

- A **banished** night (everyone in the Internet at once) is not a
  reinstallation and never locks.
- A player's booth (Clippy, an Antivirus) is never locked.
- A locked guest's Codex is refused, so the tent's printed QR works only
  after release, or through Run's codex grant.

## Restricted

A director can **restrict** a program from Run: the app greys out and the
title bar turns ochre. The guest guide says only that *the computer* may
restrict you. See [Performer station](performers.md), *A guest who is
restricted*.

## The reinstallation station and the games

- During the night only a marked (or corrupted) program may play. Any
  other pass reads **PROGRAM NOT MARKED** (*Not marked for
  reinstallation.*). A program a director held without a mark reads
  **Program held**. A program unmarked while it walks (a director
  released it) is stopped: *No longer marked for reinstallation — the
  simulation ended.*
- A marked program that isn't in the Internet yet can still scan at the
  tent: **Play here** starts its reinstallation and sends it to the
  Internet all the same.
- **The path to Gerald.** For the first minute or so of each walk the
  games show no trail, compass or distance: the HUD reads just *FIND
  GERALD*. Then a short notice comes up and the golden trail draws at
  once. A re-scan starts the minute again. Gerald's quack is never
  delayed.
- **A party favour.** Once the night is over (any ending), the games are
  open to every program: *🎉 Party favour unlocked*, nobody is captured
  by starting, and runs after the end never count for the duck speedrun
  award.
- **A banished night.** A find shows **GERALD CAN'T HELP** (no egg, no
  fanfare) on the tablet, the headset and the walls. The way out is the
  hand jive, together.
- **Headsets and terminals** are set up from the editor's help (*Setting
  up terminals, the door sensor and headsets*), never from a guest's Help.

## The King

His health bar is **one bar for every program**, all night (15 points,
never regenerating). In both games a program may hit him **once per
reinstallation** (until its duck is found, whatever re-scans happen in
between); at 0 he kneels and the door he guards in the games opens. A
host can stand him back up at full health. The bar over his head is the
only King display in the games; the web HUD carries no King meter.

Why hit him: the real chess game is on the **Server Door** terminal, and
**his bar sets his strength** there. At full health he is a grandmaster;
every hit weakens him, and a felled King plays nothing but his pawns.
A checkmate before 📄 truth.txt puts *The Door Code* in the winner's
Codex; after it the board says *No prize now.* The attract screen names
no prize. A restricted program can't challenge him. The full rules:
*Running the server room* in the editor's help.

## The Server Room terminal (his heart)

The terminal inside the Server Room is Trabolta's **heart**, and the
guest guide never calls it that.

- **The first time the door opens**, the screen tears open in red, his
  heart wakes, and he asks for one thing: **close the door**. Nothing
  happens in there with the door open; a **CLOSE THE DOOR** banner runs
  while it stands open.
- **Shut it**, and the lock clunks, the hum drops, and he asks to see a
  **pass**.
- Then he asks his questions in turn — who you are, what he is, what you
  came to say, and the last thing you'd ask of him. Nothing on his screen
  counts them or says how far you got. Some answers carry a tag
  like **⟦TRUSTED⟧** or **⟦PROOF⟧**: they only show when the program has
  earned them tonight. Push him the wrong way and he warns once; push
  again and he **throws them out** of his heart for the night.
- Answers from the phone count only at his heart (*Not out there*).
- **The door opens mid-talk?** Everything stops: the answers grey out
  under **CLOSE THE DOOR** and the line waits until the door is shut.

## Inside the games (never in the guest guide)

These are wave 26 additions in both games. The guest guide never
mentions them, and nobody should explain them to a guest.

- **Walking out of the house.** A player who walks far enough out
  through the mud room's outside door gets a scare, is sent back to the
  tent in the game, and Trabolta chastises them there, spoken in the game
  only (never on a phone, a booth or Run). Each attempt costs trust.
- **His heart in the game.** Once the King has fallen, the in-game
  server room shows Trabolta's heart. The first time a program reaches it
  in a night, he says a few short lines to it, in the game only (`#
  hosts` hears that it happened). Flavour only.
- **The disk.** A floppy disk sits in the game all night. Picking it up
  gives the same Codex entry the tent's QR does (Free Antivirus Trial or
  ILOVEYOU). The printed QRs stay as the backup.

## The descent (never in the guest guide)

The more often a program is reinstalled, the less the simulation holds
together. Its first walk is the clean game. From the second, the lights
stutter, something walks behind it and a prop wakes up and talks to it, with
choices. The third brings the wrong colours, a pale duck that isn't Gerald and
a grin in the air. From the fourth it can shrink or grow, meet itself, lose the
lights, and find a door that wasn't there, into a hallway longer than the house.
There is a tea party in the yard.

None of this is real to the story: nothing here changes a program's trust,
marks or fate, nothing stops it finding Gerald, and no prop knows anything
useful. If a guest asks, the house has always been like this. A director can
force a level in Run → House map (*Descent*).

## Footsteps in the house

A program walking in either game is heard on the real speakers near where it
walks: clearly in its own room, muffled through a wall or a ceiling. It works
only where there are speakers. If someone in the kitchen stops and listens,
that is the point. Never explain it.

## His voice comes apart

Late in the night Trabolta's speech starts to go: the pace and the pitch wander,
words stutter and drop out, other voices cut in. It follows the story by itself
and gets worse towards midnight. The big glitches hit hard for a few seconds at
any hour, then he snaps back. Help him in the end, or get to the full truth he
reads after an ending, and his voice comes back whole. That is the payoff, so never explain it.

Nothing he says changes: the captions and his chat lines stay exactly as written,
so a line that's hard to hear can always be read. If it ever gets in the way of
the show, a director or a host sets 🌀 His voice to **Clean**.

## Clippy's dream

- When Trabolta says *"…the Y2K BUG"* every terminal melts into Clippy's
  dream, all at once, and stays there until an ending. It replaces the ads
  and screensavers. Each screen is somewhere different in the hour, on
  purpose.
- Anything important still gets through, data moshed: cutscenes, slides,
  announcements. It wipes clean every few seconds, so wait a moment to read it.
- A terminal someone is using is left alone: a line to Trabolta, a chess game,
  the casino, a walk in the tent. Scan a pass as usual; the scan box is still
  in its corner, and so is the clock.
- The games' terminals keep showing the real rooms, with spurts of the dream
  breaking through every so often.
- The director's **🌀 Clippy's dream: on / off** puts everything back (and
  brings it back) if the wifi struggles or a scene must read clean.

## Terminals and the simulation

- A terminal shows and hears the game only while a reinstalling player
  is **in that terminal's room** in the game: its world view, its camera
  into the game, its microphone and speaker. The TV follows the same
  rule for the Desktop. The tent laptop is never linked. Dedicated
  mirror and shadow screens are always on.
- The **🎤** on a terminal means a player can hear that room right now:
  keep secrets (the door code) away from a 🎤 terminal.
- **Clippy's wireless mic is the TV's sound in the games.** A program
  standing in the Desktop in either game, the tent's included, hears it
  from the TV, with no 🎤 anywhere to say so. Anything said into it is
  heard, between scenes too: use the pack's mute.
- An older tablet that can't draw the 3D house shows a blocky old-school
  view of its room instead.
- A terminal that suddenly shows its own **name** full screen for ten
  seconds, with a chime, is someone pressing *identify* in Run.
- Trabolta can put a card of his own on the wall terminals for a little
  while (a dancing baby, the hamster dance), never over a story slide and
  never on the TV.
- **The idle show** is calm until the 📺 ad hijack: no popup storms, a
  popup every half minute or so. After the hijack the walls stay
  hijacked and busy.
- **The first glitch** (📺, wave 26) takes over every terminal and every
  phone once, live: the phones' takeover swallows taps for a few
  seconds; a host booth gets a short look-only version; the director
  phone and Run never do. A reload never plays it again.
- **His holding line** (*Jean Trabolta is not responding…*) is the
  system's, not a reply: the real answer still comes, from his voice or
  from a host answering as him.
- **House announcements** come out of the house speakers, and the
  terminals out of earshot say them too (Run → Terminals' 📢 list).

## Wallet

- **The audit.** Every third transfer anyone at the party sends is held
  for the auditor (CRA Agent Software): its card counts down from five
  minutes, and nobody auditing in that time means it goes through. The
  others are pending for a minute and settle unless denied. The guest
  guide says only that *some* transfers are flagged.
- **The reserve.** One program may hold **💰 Stimulus** (mint coin for
  everyone, a few times a night), **🎁 Grant** (mint coin into one
  program's account) and **🧊 Freeze** (freeze one program's coin for ten
  minutes). The guest guide describes the three buttons for whoever holds
  them, but not who that is, how many there are or when they open.
- A payment or request a director cancels reads *↩️ Director cancelled …*
  on both phones; the guest guide calls it the house cancelling.

## Sides, factions and the Codex

- At *Trapped in the Internet* the sides open at the 🕚 Y2K clock, and
  founding opens at the 📥 Truther Download; the majority count starts at
  the 11:00 PM clock. The story tracks heat on programs who switch sides.
- Directors can rename or close a guest's crew.
- A wrong Codex code is noticed by the story.
- Trabolta won't take a secret file, or lore about someone or somewhere
  else.
- Directors can read every private thread, and a character's thread is
  answered by whoever plays them tonight (or an agent).

## The end of the night

**The full truth comes first** (wave 58). Every ending plays out, and then a host
or a director presses **G0 📜 The full truth**: the whole story of the night, in
the living room, 12 to 14 minutes. Trabolta reads his own file on the TV on the
verdict (⚖ plays it, and the ending and the rave follow by themselves); Task Manager reads it off the teleprompter after
Combo A (a message he left before he died) and Combo B (the last thing the
computer saved). His voice stays clean through it, unless a director breaks it.

- **A dance track never ducks under him.** Press ■ Stop house sounds before 📜 if
  the dance song is still up, and before G1 on every ending.
- **No voice on the TV?** Mute it, then **📜 Task Manager reads it instead**. His
  phone down? His printed copy, or ⊞ Start → My script on any phone signed in to
  the part.
- **A program who didn't come tonight** is left out of it, and stays in the
  review.
- **G1** waits until it should be over. A press sooner asks first, and a second
  inside 30 s starts the ceremony anyway.
- **After Combo A he does not come back:** no motorcycle, his messages stop, and
  Clippy hosts every award.

Nothing said at his heart ends the night any more: a program who says all they
came to say is heard, and the terminal goes back to sleep.

After the awards a director may send one last alert: *🎃 The show is
over: you are human again. Hang out. The night is yours.* Every mark,
capture, lock and restriction comes off at once, and the party carries on.
