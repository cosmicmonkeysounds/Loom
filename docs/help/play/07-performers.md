The performer station is the same chat surface, recomposed for the
booth: every guest is a conversation, and your character is the mask.

## Signing in

Scan your **cast card** (or the host's performer QR): it opens the
performer sign-in with the passcode filled in. Typing it works too —
pick **I'm a Performer** and enter the **performer passcode** from the
host. The story's **parts** appear — every
character with something to do, with their pictures — tap yours. A part
is one booth's: a greyed-out name is already taken by another
performer. You now speak *as* that character everywhere.

Phone died, or you need to swap to a loaner? Your part is still held by
the old phone, so ask the director to **release** it (Run → the
character's Inspector → **⏏ Release**). Then sign in on the new phone
with the performer code and tap your part again: every guest thread and
button is where you left it. A booth whose part was released (or that
the show's going live signed out) says *"Your booth was signed out (a
director released your part, or the show went live) — sign in again with
your passcode."* A phone that wakes late in the night gets back every
thread with your guests, your cast thread and every card still waiting;
only the busy rooms are cut to their last few hundred lines.

## Scanning guests

The pinned **Scanner** thread is your instrument:

- **📷 Use camera** — point it at a guest's QR pass. Works on iPhone
  and Android; the camera stays on, so a queue scans pass after pass
  (each one shows a ✓ with the guest's id). It needs the https address.
- Or type their guest id (it's under their QR).

A scan fires your character's story for that guest — the **Readouts**
card shows what the system delivered, and anything the story wants you
to *say or do* arrives as your direction. You can also **📡 Scan this
guest** from inside their thread.

## Alerts in your booth

Your booth chimes, buzzes and shows a banner for an **alert** sent to
everyone, to your character, or into a staff room (`# hosts`), and for
any readout you didn't ask for — "⏰ *Name* is late", a private warning.
An alert the story sends to **one guest** or **one room** (a guest's own
pinned *🚨 GO TO THE INTERNET*, the lights going out in a room) doesn't
banner your booth: your cue is the readout. Tap the app once after signing in
so it may make sound (iPhones never buzz — watch for the banner). Keep
the phone awake with **☰ → Keep my screen on**; a banner you slept
through is still there when you wake it.

On an Android phone, tap **🔔 Get alerts → Turn on** (or **☰ → Get
alerts on this phone**) and allow notifications: house-wide alerts and
alerts pinned in a staff room, the server-room (patch-bay) alarm, the late list and a
transfer waiting for your approval then reach you as notifications
even with the phone locked in your pocket; tapping one opens where it
landed (a readout opens your Scanner). An alert the story sends to
**one guest** — their own pinned *🚨 GO TO THE INTERNET* — never pushes
your booth: your cue is the readout. On an iPhone, if the card shows
**Get alerts on your iPhone**: tap Share → **Add to Home Screen**, then
**☰ → Sign out** (that frees your part) and sign in from the Home
Screen icon (a part is one phone's; a director's ⏏ Release frees one
that's stuck), and tap 🔔 there. If it says **Your iPhone can't get alerts**, keep the screen on.

## Pinned lines, and a guest who is restricted

A room can carry a **pinned line** above its conversation — a standing
notice the story put there, up until the story clears it. At *Trapped
in the Internet* the `# quarantine` room pins one escort task per
program marked for reinstallation (*🚨 Escort Excel to the Internet —
chose Dare…*), and it comes down with a ✅ when the program is captured,
released, or unmarked.

A guest the story has **restricted** shows a **⛔ restricted** pill on
their card and on their thread's line in your guest list: their phone is
greyed out, its buttons are off, they can't post in rooms or check in,
and they keep only their own feed (with the pinned 🚨 line), their
private messages, their pinned rooms and the places the story named. Their
thread with you still works — talk to them there. In the show, that is
a marked program: your readout says why, and the escort is yours.

## Judging filed facts

Guests hand a character an answer on a **📝 File a fact** card instead
of saying it (see [Filing a fact](quests.md)): a question, the answer,
and a confidence slider. The **server judges it** the moment it is
filed, against the story's answer key — *accurate*, a *confident lie*
(wrong at 100%), or *uncertain* (wrong below) — and the story reacts at
once; nothing waits for a host. The one exception is a **near miss**: an
answer close to the key but not exact (*Dr Whiskars* for *Doctor Whiskers*, a number written
out, the right answer with a few more words). An abbreviation (*Dr.
Whiskers*) is simply accurate. It is never judged a lie or a guess by itself: it is *proposed
accurate*, a ⚠ line in `# hosts` names the answer it is close to, and it
waits for a person (you, with the buttons below, or the director's
Judging card). At *Trapped in the Internet* Trabolta rules on it first
when his laptop is online: `# hosts` says *… asked Trabolta to rule*,
then *🤖 Trabolta ruled …* with his verdict and reason, and the story
reacts as if a host had pressed. You only step in when you disagree.
If nobody judges it, it counts as accurate when the round closes. At *Trapped in the Internet* the
`# hosts` room shows each filing as it lands ("📝 *Name* filed H5 (round
2 Q2): *answer* — certain. Judged lie."). The story's buttons on that guest's card —
**📝 Filed fact: accurate** / **confident lie** / **uncertain (a
guess)** — are the **override**: press one when the key is wrong or a
spelling was unfair, and the story takes your word over the judge's.
Your button judges that guest's **latest** filing. If they have
filed more than once and an earlier one is still waiting, *Trapped in
the Internet* turns your press down (*📝 Not judged: … has an earlier
filing still waiting for a verdict. Judge it on Run → Mind → Judging …*)
and changes nothing: ask the director, whose Judging card names each
filing's question and can override any of them.
When the director has switched judging to *review*, every verdict is a
proposal until one of those buttons (or the director) confirms it. The
guest gets the same reply either way — only the story knows what the
verdict was.

## Your character's own rooms

Under **Pinned** your booth has the character's own **📜 Quest Log**
(the story can hand your character quests — a staff brief, step by
step, in its own thread each), its **📓 Codex** (what your character
knows, and can share from a guest's card), a **Wallet** when your
character audits the story's currency (below), and an **Inbox**: cards
and notes the story deals to your character. Answer a card in the
Inbox like a guest would — a poll, a yes / no — and the story hears it
*as your character*.

## Approving transfers

If your character is the story's **auditor** (at *Trapped in the
Internet*, CRA Agent Software), the header shows a badge with how many
transfers are waiting on you, and your **Wallet** room lists them as
cards: *🧾 Excel → Winamp: 40 Truthcoin. Waiting for your approval* with
**Approve** / **Deny**, or *settles in 42 s unless you deny it* with
**Deny** alone. Every third transfer anyone sends waits for your
Approve (or goes through by itself after five minutes, if you never
answer); every other one settles by itself at the end of its window
unless you deny it first. A denial is refunded to the sender and the
card says you did it. Trades of lore for coin never pass you; a request
someone pays does. A held transfer pushes your phone even when it is
locked (Android, with alerts on). Elaborate security questions are
encouraged; the story cannot freeze anyone's balance for you.

## Guest threads and the guest card

Each guest is a thread under **Guests**. What you type there lands as
your character's DM to them. The **broadcast feed** thread posts to the
main room — everyone hears it.

Everything you can *do to* a guest lives on their **card**: open it with
**⚡ Actions** at the top of their thread, by tapping their name in any
room, or from a room's 👥 who's-here list. The card shows where they
are and their group, and holds **📡 Scan**, **📓 Share lore…**, and the
**story's own buttons** — whatever the writers declared for performers
(a whisper, a blessing, a search…). Pressing one acts *as your
character*, on that guest. The card leads with **your** buttons that
make sense right now; the rest (anyone's, or yours for a later part of
the night) fold under **More…** — still there if you need them. A button
the story gives to **another** character only (the server-room watch's
patch-bay buttons, a host's verdict) isn't on your card at all: the
server would refuse it from your booth (*not your button …*).
Rooms themselves stay clean: just the conversation and your composer.

**Some buttons take two taps.** A button with no way back — one that
ends the night, trips an alarm, hands over a secret or plays a
once-a-night moment — **arms** on the first tap (it turns black and
says *Tap again to fire*) and fires on the second. Tap again within
four seconds; a very fast double tap only arms it. Leave it and it
disarms by itself. The guest's own buttons on their pass work the same
way when the story marks them so.

## Rooms

Every place is a room, and the list says who's standing in each — the
same view the guests have, minus the "you are here" (a character is
everywhere). Authored channels work like the guest app: post as your
character, invite guests into gated rooms, leave when the scene's done.
Lines the story sends to one guest privately (their arrival, a whisper)
are theirs alone; you see only what your character says or what plays
to a room.

## Moderator powers

If the host gave you the **moderator passcode**, open **☰ → Become an
admin**. Admins additionally can:

- **🔒 Capture / 🔓 Release** any guest from their thread (for stories
  that use it).
- **Hide/show** any message in any thread (moderation).

Use it like stage management, not like power — the story's operator can
see everything you do.
